The services we run

Three servers, each with one job:

  • setup.tuxblox.netWhich version your release channel serves and the file list for it, TuxBlox's own downloads, and the list of builds the releases page shows
  • geo.tuxblox.netThe region of a Roblox server you joined, answered from a database held on that same machine
  • telemetry.tuxblox.netCrash reports, when one is sent

Roblox's own files never come from us. The launcher downloads the Roblox client, and checks which Roblox version is current, from Roblox's servers. Those requests are between you and Roblox, under Roblox's terms.

What we ask of you

The services are small, free, and paid for by the people who run them. Keeping them up for everybody means a short list of things not to do with them:

  • Do not use them as general purpose infrastructure. The region lookup answers questions about Roblox servers, not about any address you care to send it
  • Do not automate bulk downloads, and do not point another project's updater at our download server
  • Do not send crash reports that are not crash reports, and do not flood the endpoint with invented ones
  • Do not attack, probe or try to get into the servers, and do not interfere with anybody else's use of them
  • Do not use them to do anything that is illegal where you are

Reporting a security vulnerability privately is not a breach of these terms, and we will take no action against you for it. No block, no claim, nothing. That holds even if it turns out you were wrong about what you found.

Finding a hole is not probing in the sense above, as long as you report it rather than use it. Reporting a Security Issue is how to do that, and it is the policy these terms point to: it says what is in scope, what a report should contain, and where to send it.

What this does not cover is using what you found, taking a service down to prove it, or reaching into somebody else's data. Those are the things the list above is about.

Rate limits and blocking

We may rate limit a request, refuse it, or block an address altogether. When something is actively hurting a service we will do it immediately and without warning, because the alternative is the service going down for everybody.

There is no account, so a block lands on an address rather than on a person, and it is not a judgement about you. Addresses are shared and reassigned, which means a block can catch somebody who did nothing — that is a limitation of having nothing to sign in to, not an intention.

If you think you have been blocked wrongly, email contact@tuxblox.net and we will look.

Availability and changes

The services are free and best effort. There is no uptime figure here, no promise that one answers tomorrow, and no promise that it stays at the same address or keeps answering the same way. Any of them can be taken down for maintenance, moved, changed or withdrawn.

TuxBlox is built so that none of that stops you using what you already have. An update check that cannot finish does not prevent a launch, and the Roblox you have installed runs whether or not our servers answer.

We do not promise that any particular release stays downloadable. If a version matters to you, keep your own copy of it.
